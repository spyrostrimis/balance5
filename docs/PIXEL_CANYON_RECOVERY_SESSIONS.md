# Pixel Canyon · Choosing how to build the moving environment

**Recommendation.** Give Sol Session A first. Judge the hardest few seconds of the walk, where the commander passes a near wall and the terrace opens into view. Session B is the alternative if a fully 2D map is a priority. Session C is the alternative if the painted viewpoint is essential and a fixed camera track is acceptable. Each prompt is self-contained. Choose a branch after watching its footage.

## What happened in Study III

The commander and the road share a working sense of distance. The road is drawn from ground coordinates each frame. The old run log records the boots between Y 317 and 333 with leeway enabled and a return to the starting ground point. Retain these as comparison values. [Study and measurements](PIXEL_CANYON_FOLLOW_STUDY.md) · [Run log](../render_captures/pixel_canyon_follow/leeway.log)

The scenery was constructed differently. Each stair-and-wall group, ledge or ruin was traced from a finished painting, placed by a single point, and enlarged as a unit. In the painting, its nearest step, far parapet and supporting cliff are at different distances. Approaching them should change their relative positions and uncover surfaces that were hidden. Uniform enlargement preserves the old overlap. It also enlarges the painted pixels, which are conspicuously coarse on the nearest wall beside the sharp commander. The ground shader supplies the shoulder, rim and a broad shadow with no knowledge of the exact foot of a wall. [Cutout preparation](../tools/python/build_pixel_canyon_follow_art.py) · [Placement and scaling](../scripts/pixel_canyon/pixel_follow_art.gd) · [Ground drawing](../assets/shaders/pixel_canyon/ground.gdshader)

At spawn the left stair foundation ends in bare dirt. Inside the bend the same stepped rock and brush shapes occupy both shoulders, sometimes mirrored with their painted sunlight. At the overlook the rounded underside of the left terrace is visible against the valley. In the source, the staircase runs into paving and a cliff face carries the weight of the platform. That missing junction is the first thing the next study should build. [Spawn](../render_captures/pixel_canyon_follow/leeway_out_spawn.png) · [Bend](../render_captures/pixel_canyon_follow/leeway_out_bend.png) · [Overlook](../render_captures/pixel_canyon_follow/leeway_out_overlook.png) · [Original overlook](../assets/textures/pixel_canyon/arch_overlook.png)

The rough walk answered the camera question. It used abstract scenery, so it left the art question open. A full-length, low-angle walk needs newly visible surfaces from somewhere. The two fixed paintings alone supply too few views for the near walls. They remain strong references for composition, colour and lighting, and useful source art for the distant skyline. If modest travel between painted compositions becomes acceptable, Study II already demonstrates the lower-cost option of a short pan. [Camera and Depth](PIXEL_CANYON_CAMERA_STUDY.md)

## Session A · A solid bend with the painted commander

> Work in D:/Games/Balance5. Build one standalone comparison of a 2D Blue Commander walking past a solid 3D canyon wall into Arch Overlook. Read AGENTS.md, the three Pixel Canyon study reports and the canyon visual rubric and experiment log. Compare the original arch_overlook.png to render_captures/pixel_canyon_follow/leeway_out_overlook.png. The target is the exact place where the left staircase, terrace foundation and road belong together.
>
> Borrow the route, containment and following-camera work from scenes/maps/canyon_v2.tscn as useful. Frame the existing pixel commander at about 125 art pixels tall in a 960×540 game image. Aim for the low view, warm upper-right light, warm cobbles and pale valley of the two paintings. Use a single short bend, a curb that can be followed with the eye, a near sandstone wall with a visible top and side, a terrace physically supported by the cliff, and a twin arch beyond. The foreground wall, steps and foundation need actual thickness. A painted distant sky and valley are sufficient. Include one representative finished stretch where curb, steps, rock and shadow meet so the owner can judge the pixel style as well as the structure.
>
> Record a short Forward+ walk at 1376×768. Approach the wall, pause with the terrace partly visible, reverse several steps, continue to the reveal, move sideways across the road and retrace the route. Preserve a close, readable commander and stable camera distance, allowing the small follow leeway where practical. Deliver a playable scene path, a real-time clip and matched entrance, wall and overlook frames. Put the source painting beside the overlook frame. Report separately whether the staircase stays connected, whether the arch grows convincingly and whether the materials and lighting look like the reference. End this session at the short comparison.

## Session B · One connected, fully 2D scrolling canyon

> Work in D:/Games/Balance5. Test whether an entirely 2D scrolling map can deliver the walk with a higher, fixed-angle view. Read AGENTS.md, the canyon visual rubric and experiment log, and docs/PIXEL_CANYON_FOLLOW_STUDY.md. Inspect the existing scenes/rpg_2d/canyon_orthographic_study.tscn and its bend capture before starting. Make a separate small study based on what already works there. Use the original Road to the Arch and Arch Overlook paintings as the art targets.
>
> Author a single connected stretch from a narrow bend into an overlook. Draw or tile the near cliff top, vertical face, base, terrace stairs and contact shadow together at one consistent angle and pixel scale. Continue the curb through the bend. Compose the near wall so it hides the overlook until the commander rounds it. The arch can come into view at the map’s fixed scale. Give the commander free sideways steps and uphill/downhill travel with a following 2D camera and small leeway. Give this short junction representative colour, lighting and paving so its art can be judged.
>
> Record the approach, a stop and reversal beside the wall, a sideways crossing and the return at game speed. Supply a standalone playable scene path, the clip and matched frames at the entrance, wall and overlook. Show the same identifiable step on the way back. Put the overlook frame beside the original painting and identify the change in viewing angle, apparent depth and arch size for the owner. End at this one bend.

## Session C · A painted camera track, playable in 2D

> Work in D:/Games/Balance5. Test a short, pre-rendered approach to Arch Overlook played interactively in Godot 2D. Read AGENTS.md, the canyon visual rubric and experiment log, and docs/PIXEL_CANYON_FOLLOW_STUDY.md. Use the two original paintings as targets. Choose just the few seconds when the near wall uncovers the left terrace and the twin arch.
>
> Build or reuse one common layout for every view, using the Canyon v2 route or the Session A bend if available. Render a closely spaced series of views along one camera track with a consistent pixel-art treatment. The foreground wall, terrace foundation, road and arch must agree across views. Drive view selection from the player’s distance along the path. Stopping must freeze the view, and walking back must retrace exactly the same views. Draw the existing 2D commander live. Allow sideways movement across the road while the camera stays on its track. Supply a foreground mask or depth pass per view so a near pillar or wall covers her correctly. Test her boots at both road edges.
>
> Deliver the playable excerpt and a real-time recording of slow walking, stopping, reversing and crossing sideways at the reveal. Include three reference-matched stills. Report the number of frames, stored size, visible stepping at slow speed, and how much artwork required individual correction. State how those measured costs would scale to the full walk. End with whether this technique buys enough painted fidelity to justify preparing the rest of the track.

## After the comparison

Choose A if the bend reads as solid in motion and the finished patch feels like the painting. If structure succeeds and colour or texture misses, the next experiment is a material and lighting pass on that same patch. Choose B if its connected painting and higher angle feel right for the game. Choose C if the low-angle painted look wins and stops, reversals and memory cost hold up. Expand the chosen method to the two-room route and restore the scout encounter after the visual decision. Keep the current studies available as comparisons.
