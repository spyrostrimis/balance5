# Pixel Canyon III · Through the Bend

**Implementation brief for Sol.** Build a third, standalone, playable Godot 2D study. The Blue Commander can walk from Road to the Arch, past the scout and around the bend, into Arch Overlook, then retrace every step. The viewer travels with her in both directions. The feeling to aim for is that the arch gets closer while she stays close enough to read her scarf, rifle and stride.

## What carries forward

The [Two-Room Study](PIXEL_CANYON_STUDY.md) supplies the two places, the boot-anchored walk, shrub crossing and scout encounter. [Camera and Depth](PIXEL_CANYON_CAMERA_STUDY.md) supplies the 960×540 framing, the 125-art-pixel standing commander and the measured relationship between her height and nearby paving. Its C variant pans up to 24 art pixels in the first room. Here the camera needs to travel the length of the route in either direction.

Use the existing studies as playable comparisons. The old gate calls `_enter_room()`, frees the current room and places the commander at a new spawn. The old depth curve also ties sprite height to a fixed painting’s Y coordinate. Give the new study its own world controller and apply depth relative to the moving view. Reuse the sprite sheet, boot pivot, walk and fire poses, native viewport and display controls. [Room controller](../scripts/pixel_canyon/pixel_study.gd) · [Commander](../scripts/pixel_canyon/pixel_commander.gd) · [Display scene](../scenes/pixel_canyon/pixel_canyon.tscn)

## Build order

1. **Prove the walk with rough art.** Create `scenes/pixel_canyon/pixel_canyon_follow.tscn`. Lay out one continuous ground path with a sideways coordinate and a distance along the canyon. Mark spawn, shrub, scout, bend and twin arch. Give the bend enough length for the near wall to pass across the view and for the overlook to open beyond it. Start with plain road bands, curb markers and arch silhouettes. Walk up, stop halfway through the bend and back out. Judge this pass at game speed before painting.

2. **Give the camera a small amount of leeway.** Start with the original boots at screen (511, 325) and the original idle height of 125 art pixels. As a first tuning value, let the boots move 8 art pixels above or below Y 325. When they reach either edge of that band, advance or retreat the camera by the excess. On reversal, the boots cross the band before the view follows the other way. Keep sideways movement free across the road. Carry the framing around the bend with the route. Provide a tuning value of zero for a direct comparison with exact follow. Use fractional positions for movement and round the final drawing positions to native pixels. On release, the camera should settle immediately. Allow enough scenery beyond both playable ends to keep this framing there.

3. **Make the ground convey forward motion.** One workable 2D technique is to draw the road in short crosswise strips. For each strip, use its distance ahead of the camera to determine its screen height, apparent width and cobble size. Use the same mapping for the feet of the scout, shrub, walls and arches. Cobble patterns belong to fixed places on the route so the same stones pass back underfoot on the return. Match the original standing view first, including the roughly 24-pixel cobbles around the boots. Keep the commander near her original height throughout, with only the small scale change warranted by the leeway. Check that a distant arch grows into a nearby landmark and that the ground moves in step with planted boots.

4. **Fit the two paintings to the route.** Use the road, ruins, shrub and warm upper-right light from the approach, and the open left-hand vista and twin arch from the overlook. Both paintings contain their own horizon and a road receding into the distance. Reuse sections at their appropriate depth. Separate the distant skyline from the nearest wall and road, paint the short connecting bend, and fill the areas that become visible behind the moving foreground. Let the near wall briefly hide the change of vista while the road, curb and commander remain continuous. Check the reveal downhill as carefully as uphill. Keep the source art sharp and use nearest-neighbour sampling.

5. **Reconnect gameplay on the continuous ground.** Put the walkable edges, shrub roots, scout, shots and commander’s boots in the same gameplay coordinates. Draw the projected view from those positions each frame. Keep free movement in depth and left/right facing and firing. The scout holds the bend until hit. Clearing the scout opens the path in place and persists on the return. Keep shadow contact, overlap at the shrub, shot lanes and rifle muzzle alignment correct as the view moves. Keep controls on a fixed screen layer.

## Evidence for review

- **One uninterrupted round trip.** Record spawn → shrub → scout → bend → twin arch → spawn at game speed. Pause and reverse halfway through the join. A viewer should be able to follow the same curb or rock back through it, with continuous input and no cut, fade, teleport or camera reset.
- **Matched frames.** Capture spawn, both sides of the shrub, inside the bend and the overlook in both travel directions. Log route position, camera position, on-screen boot Y and standing sprite height. With the proposed leeway, boot Y should stay within 317–333, allowing a pixel for rounding. Compare idle poses across the route and review moving footage for sliding boots, shimmering cobbles, doubled horizons and exposed artwork edges.
- **Gameplay checks.** Run a headless walk that attempts the blocked bend, misses off-lane, hits on-lane, crosses the former room boundary in both directions and returns to the defeated scout. Exercise stops, quick reversals and lateral movement against both road edges. Re-run the existing study checks if shared code changes.
- **Playable handoff.** Capture the real Forward+ viewport at 1376×768 with the 960×540 game image. Save this study’s evidence under `render_captures/pixel_canyon_follow/`. Add a separate **Play** card on the Developers Page, verify launch and Esc return, and re-export the Windows build. Record the chosen follow setting and verified results in the study notes and relevant project memory.

The first review decision is whether the rough walk through the bend feels like approaching a place while staying beside the commander. Use that recording to choose exact follow or a little leeway before finishing the connecting art.
